## SLIDE 4: The Perficient Approach (2 minutes)

**Title:** "Hub-and-Spoke Operating Model"

**Visual:** Simple diagram
```
        [Central Hub]
          /  |  \
       /     |     \
     BU1   BU2    BU3
```

**Key Components:**

**The Hub Owns:**
- Tool evaluation and certification
- Governance standards
- High-risk intake (with SLAs)
- Champions network
- Measurement and ROI

**Business Units Own:**
- Day-to-day execution
- Low-risk decisions (no approval gate)
- Champion activation
- Reporting results

**Talking Points:**
- "This isn't centralized control—it's distributed execution with central intelligence"
- "Speed: Low-risk decisions move instantly"
- "Governance: High-risk decisions have 48-72 hour SLAs"
- "Scale: Hub doesn't grow with adoption; champions do"

**Speaker Notes:** Emphasize that this enables speed, not blocks it. The key insight: governance is the accelerant, not the brake.

## PRESENTATION TALKING POINTS (Full Script)

### Opening (30 sec)
"We're seeing a clear pattern across our enterprise customers: shadow AI. Teams are using unsanctioned tools because the sanctioned path is too slow. It's not a compliance problem—it's a speed problem. And today I want to show you a framework we've built to solve it."

### Problem (1 min)
"Here's what's happening: A researcher needs to analyze competitive intelligence. She has two options. Option A: submit a request, wait 5-7 days for approval, then use a tool. Option B: open ChatGPT and get the answer in 5 minutes. She picks B. Every time. Not because she's reckless—because she has a job to do. And that's the insight: the fastest path and the safest path are pointing in different directions. So what most companies do is tighten governance, add more gates, try to block shadow AI with policies. It doesn't work. In fact, it makes it worse. Because now teams have two choices: slow and sanctioned, or fast and unsanctioned. So we've built something different."

### Solution (2 min)
"Instead of centralized control, we use a hub-and-spoke model. The hub sets standards, evaluates tools, runs governance, and measures impact. Business units execute with approved tools. Champions—influential leaders in each BU—drive adoption locally. Here's what makes it work: Low-risk decisions move instantly. No approval gate. High-risk decisions have structured review with clear SLAs: 48-72 hours, not indefinite waits. And critically: we make the sanctioned path *faster* than the unsanctioned one. We use two-track intake. Champions can deploy approved tools in days. For risky stuff, we have a structured process with SLAs. We also train people through the role they play—we've identified 7 personas, and each has a 4-session training arc. Tool access is unlocked by training completion, not just seat licenses. And we give people personal budgets: $80 a month, say, with daily consumption alerts. Makes the cost salient. Prevents runaway use."

### Business Case (1 min)
"Why is this valuable to us? Because every enterprise is wrestling with this problem. We've built a proven framework. We can now sell this as a service. 90-day implementation, $250-350K per engagement, measurable outcomes. Conservative projection: 3-5 pilot engagements Year 1, $1-1.5M revenue. Year 2+: $3-5M as this becomes a repeatable offering. This is not a side project—this is a new business line."

### The Ask (1 min)
"So here's what I'm asking: One, approve this as a new service offering. Two, let me staff it: one full-time engagement lead, half-time governance specialist. Three, help me identify 2-3 pilot customers—we already know 10+ companies asking about this. If you say yes, I can have the sales team briefed in a week, first proposal out in two weeks, first customer engaged in three weeks. This is moving fast because the market window is closing. Competitors are building frameworks like this. Let's own this space."

## PRESENTER TIPS

**Before the Meeting:**
- Practice this 3-4 times (should take 10-12 min)
- Know the demo cold—no stumbles
- Have the memo printed or on a tablet to pass around
- Have talking points on note cards (not the full script)

**During the Presentation:**
- Open with confidence: "We have a solution to a billion-dollar problem"
- Use the problem statement to make them nod ("Yeah, we see this")
- Show the demo without apologizing ("This is a prototype, but it's real")
- Make the ask crystal clear (not vague)
- End with urgency ("We have 6 months, not years")

**Handling Questions:**
- **"Why haven't competitors done this?"** → "Some are starting to. That's why we need to move now."
- **"How do you know it works?"** → "We validated the model across [mention your customer data/experience]. The prototype proves feasibility."
- **"What if a pilot fails?"** → "We de-risk with customer selection (pick teams ready to adopt) and clear measurement (results in 90 days)."
- **"What's the competition?"** → "Most consulting firms are still selling generic 'AI strategy.' We're selling operational governance."

**After the Meeting:**
- Send the memo to everyone who attended
- Offer to brief sales team that week
- Get specific commitment on pilot customer targets
